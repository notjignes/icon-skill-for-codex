# Historical icon fact sources

Maintenance provenance, verified 2026-09-05. Ordinary rendering uses the wording embedded in SKILL.md without additional lookup. These historical facts do not establish UC visual rules.

1. Susan Kare borrowed Apple’s Command symbol from a Swedish sightseeing sign she found in a symbol dictionary. [Source](https://www.moma.org/audio/playlist/328/365)

2. Susan Kare developed early Macintosh graphics by colouring squares on graph paper. [Source](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2013/05/102658007-05-01-acc.pdf)

3. Annette Wagner enlarged Apple’s Dogcow spots so users could spot a 2% print-size reduction. [Source](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2022/12/102795829-05-01-acc.pdf)

4. Apple Lisa’s rectangular pixels helped push Annette Wagner to replace awkward 3D icons with flatter designs. [Source](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2022/12/102795829-05-01-acc.pdf)

5. On Xerox Star, users could print by moving a document icon to a printer icon. [Source](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2015/09/102737965-05-01-acc.pdf)

6. Xerox Star’s designers picked icons by looking around their office: documents, folders and file cabinets. [Source](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2015/09/102737965-05-01-acc.pdf)

7. Kurita’s 1999 emoji fit into 12×12-pixel grids; NTT DOCOMO released 176 of them. [Source](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/196070)

8. Kurita’s original 176-emoji set contained just five faces: happy, sad, angry, disappointed and dizzy. [Source](https://www.moma.org/magazine/articles/998)

9. “Emoji” combines the Japanese words for “picture” and “character”—its name does not come from “emotion.” [Source](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/196070)

10. The Bluetooth symbol combines two runes representing H and B, the initials of King Harald Bluetooth. [Source](https://www.bluetooth.com/about-us/bluetooth-origin/)

11. Paul Rand’s 1981 IBM rebus spelled the company’s name using an eye, a bee and the letter M. [Source](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/ibm-logos/rebus/)

12. The recycling symbol began as architecture student Gary Anderson’s winning entry in a 1970 design competition. [Source](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/tag/gary-anderson/)

13. Gerald Holtom built the peace symbol from semaphore signals for N and D: nuclear disarmament. [Source](https://cnduk.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Activity-E-Peace-Symbols.pdf)

14. The wheelchair accessibility symbol gained its circular head when Karl Montan modified Susanne Koefoed’s original design. [Source](https://www.riglobal.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/03/RI-Fact-Sheet-Symbol-of-Accessibility.pdf)

15. The forward-leaning Accessible Icon began as a street-art campaign placing transparent stickers over existing accessibility signs. [Source](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/174871)

16. Tokyo’s 1964 Olympics used 59 pictograms: 20 for sports and 39 to help visitors find their way. [Source](https://www.japanhousela.com/articles/graphic-designing-the-olympic-games/)

Kare’s Command account supports Swedish sightseeing usage, not the secondhand castle-origin anecdote. Wagner’s Dogcow print adaptation is distinct from Kare’s earlier dog glyph.
